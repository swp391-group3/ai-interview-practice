# RoleCue brand decision

## Why RoleCue

RoleCue names the product's two-part promise without overstating what it does. **Role** keeps the experience anchored to a specific job and technical context. **Cue** describes the timely prompts, turn-taking, preparation, and feedback that help someone show up ready for that role. It is concise, legible in English, and works naturally across the product journey: analyze a role, receive cues, rehearse, review, and try again.

RoleCue is the active working design name. This workspace makes no claim about trademark, company-name, domain, or legal availability.

## Brand attributes

- Precise and calm: clear hierarchy, intentional space, no visual noise.
- Professional and technically credible: editorial restraint over sci-fi decoration.
- Human: the system helps a person prepare and respond; it is not personified as a robot.
- Premium and modern: near-white surfaces, near-black type, and a single measured green accent.
- Progressive: the identity implies readiness and a next response without promising an automated outcome.

## Rejected cliché directions

- Robot heads, sparkles, neural meshes, or synthetic faces that make the experience feel generic or impersonal.
- Microphones, camera lenses, or waveform marks that reduce the product to a single interview medium.
- Chat bubbles, typing dots, and headset motifs that make the mark read as support software.
- Neon gradients, cyberpunk contrast, mascots, or gaming-style emblems that conflict with a calm technical-practice environment.

## Direction exploration

1. **Split Halo — selected.** An open ring creates a focus field; a short green diagonal tick signals the moment to enter, respond, or progress.
2. **Editorial Brackets — rejected.** Two offset brackets hinted at selection and dialogue, but felt too much like quotation markup at small sizes.
3. **Forward Path — rejected.** A stepped line communicated progression, but resembled a generic productivity or project-management symbol.

## Final logo concept

The selected **Split Halo** is an asymmetric open halo in near-black, interrupted by a restrained green cue tick. The empty center is deliberate: it keeps the mark focused rather than literal. The halo holds the idea of role focus and readiness; the tick provides one visible moment of direction and turn-taking. It can also be read as a quiet, abstract “C” without turning into a literal initial.

The companion wordmark is a custom, open-stroke geometric drawing. Its modest weight and generous counters carry the editorial tone while remaining clear at common product-header sizes.

## Support for the interview product

The mark can sit at the beginning of the journey as a role-focus signal, stay unobtrusive during the live interview, and return in feedback as a sign of the next practice cue. This makes the identity useful across setup, preflight, interview, review, and repeat practice without assigning it a literal feature meaning.
